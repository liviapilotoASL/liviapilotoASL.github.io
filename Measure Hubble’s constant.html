Measure the expansion of the universe

Galaxies are moving away from us, and the farther away they are, the faster they go. You will measure the distance and the redshifted wavelength of galaxies. Write your measurements down, then use them to work out each galaxy’s speed and make your graph on your worksheet.

1. Choose a galaxy

Click a galaxy in this deep-field view. Smaller ones are farther away.

2. Measure its distance

All these galaxies are really about the same size, so a galaxy that looks smaller is farther away. Resize the galaxy until its outer edge just touches the dashed circle.

3. Measure its speed

Calcium in a galaxy absorbs light at 393.4 nm in the lab, shown by the ▲ marker. In a moving galaxy the dark line is shifted toward red. Click the center of the dark line to record its wavelength.
