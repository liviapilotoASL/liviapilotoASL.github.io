Redshift: reading motion from light

The dark lines in a spectrum tell you what an object is made of. They also tell you how fast it is moving. In this lab you will see why moving objects change the colour of their light, learn the redshift equation, and practise using it before you measure real galaxies in the Hubble’s law lab.

2. The redshift equation

The size of the shift tells you the speed. Redshift z measures how much the wavelength has been stretched, as a fraction of its original value.

z = (λobs − λlab) ÷ λlab
v = z × c

λobs is the wavelength you observe, λlab is the wavelength measured in a laboratory, and c = 300,000 km/s is the speed of light.

Worked example. The calcium line has λlab = 393.4 nm and is observed at 400.0 nm.
Shift: 400.0 − 393.4 = 6.6 nm
z = 6.6 ÷ 393.4 = 0.0168
v = 0.0168 × 300,000 ≈ 5,000 km/s, moving away.

A positive z is a redshift (moving away). A negative z is a blueshift (moving toward us). This simple equation works well when z is small, below about 0.1.
